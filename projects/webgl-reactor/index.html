WebGL Reactor

A 2D WebGL heat simulation with a brush-based material editor.

Draw a material layout on the grid, then press Simulate. During simulation, move the cursor over the grid to apply the 1,000 W heater.

200 × 200 cells · 50.000 mm per cell

The plate is 10 m × 10 m and 1 mm thick, with a 200 × 200 material grid. It starts as vacuum; paint concrete, steel, water, or air with a 1–50 cell square brush. Concrete and steel are static. Water and air carry separate heat fields that advect together in the selected direction. Flow speed moves a fraction from 0 to 1 cell per physics step, so lower settings transfer only part of the upstream heat on each step. At zero flow they behave as stationary materials. Their heat fields are hidden and do not interact behind static solids, but retain their temperatures until exposed again. New fluid entering the grid is 300 K. Heat leaving the outlet is accumulated and shown as thermal power at 40% efficiency. Static edge materials exchange heat with 300 K surroundings at 1.23 × 10⁻¹¹ × (T⁴ − 8.1 × 10⁹) W per edge cell. The heater is 3 mm × 3 mm and 1,000 W. Neutrons each add 50 W to the material cell they occupy. Press Space to pause or play.
