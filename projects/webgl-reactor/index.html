WebGL Reactor

A 2D WebGL heat simulation with a brush-based material editor.

Choose an example design or draw a material layout on the grid, then press Simulate. Neutrons spawn from U235 and Pu-239 cells every fourth physics step. Cross sections determine interaction probabilities along each neutron path. Water neutrons scatter elastically and retain a normal-like sampled fraction of 0–100% of their kinetic energy (mean 50%); air retains 75.8–100% (mean 87.9%); sodium retains 84–100% (mean 92%). Below 0.025 eV, water has a 2% capture chance and air has a 14% capture chance per interaction. Below 0.08 eV, sodium has an 18% capture chance. Concrete and steel scatter with fixed KE losses of 5.88% and 1.83%; below 0.03 eV, their capture chances are 1.7% and 17.5%. Lead-208 always scatters with a 0.485% KE loss. Cadmium-113 captures below 0.8 eV, has an 80% capture chance between 0.8 eV and 10 keV, and always scatters above 10 keV with a 0.8% KE loss. Interactions in remaining materials remain total absorption for now. The added 9Be, Zircaloy, and graphite always scatter, retaining 88.8%, 98.89%, and 91.6% of neutron KE respectively. Their total cross sections use the supplied microscopic data converted with the supplied atom number densities.

The plate is 10 m × 10 m and 1 mm thick, with a 200 × 200 material grid. It starts as vacuum; paint concrete, steel, water, air, U235, Cd-113, Pb-208, Pu-239, or Na-23 with a 1–50 cell square brush. Export TXT or upload a 200 × 200 text grid using one hex digit (0–F) per cell, with the top row first; 0 is vacuum. IDs A–F are reserved and currently simulate as insulating cells with concrete heat capacity. Concrete, steel, U235, Cd-113, Pb-208, and Pu-239 are static; water, air, and Na-23 flow at the selected speed and direction. The universal heat transport multiplier scales conduction, boundary heat loss, and fluid advection. Steps/Frame sets the number of physics steps per display frame, from 0 to 100. The Neutrons/sec control accumulates its rate between spawn checks. Spawn checks run every fourth physics step and choose random cells containing U235 or Pu-239; the live-particle cap is 4,096. Neutron energy is sampled uniformly from 1 eV to 10 MeV and sets its velocity. Absorption is sampled from the supplied macroscopic cross sections. The compact table uses a 2% adaptive error target and logarithmic hash lookup. Absorption deposits a 500 W impulse for one physics step; fission is not implemented. Neutrons are not drawn. Exported/imported material grids preserve all hexadecimal IDs. New fluid entering the grid is 300 K; heat leaving the outlet is shown as thermal power at 40% efficiency. Static edge materials exchange heat with 300 K surroundings. Steps/Frame controls neutron flight resolution as well as heat updates. Press Space to pause or play.
